Are Our Roads Safer for Everyone or Just Some?

An interactive evaluation of core transport injury registries, seasonal accident cycles, and regional health resource inequality across Australia.

01 // Injury Index over Time

Traffic Injury Trends over Time: First Nations vs Baseline

Indexing longitudinal trends from 2011 to 2021 exposes a critical equity divergence. While the broader national baseline tracks a stable trajectory, First Nations transport injuries have spiked, revealing a systemic policy failure.

02 // Age Profiles

Traffic Hospitalisations by Age Group

Disaggregating transport trauma exposes an asymmetrical public health burden. The First Nations profile exhibits an alarming concentration centered squarely within the 17–25 youth cohort.

03 // Vehicle Breakdown

Hospitalisations by Vehicle Type and Month

Evaluating hospitalisations through an Archimedean spiral heatmap exposes hidden seasonal loops across the year. We can isolate holiday surges and specific vehicle risk density.

04 // Trauma Flow

Systemic Trauma Flow: Region → Vehicle → Severity

An alluvial Sankey traces every hospitalisation from where it happened, through which vehicle type was involved, to how severe the resulting stay was. Remote pedestrian injuries concentrate disproportionately in the severe tier — a signature of delayed emergency response.
